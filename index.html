8 Key Findings

These eight statements capture the most important patterns across all measures for the 2024-26 school years. Each is drawn directly from the data and is meant to anchor the deeper charts that follow.

1
The reading gap is nearly gone — and boys closed it.
Boys' average STAR Reading score rose +4.1 points year over year while girls' rose only +0.7 points. The mean gap collapsed from 4.7 points (girls leading) to just 1.3 points. At the median, the gap is now zero.
2
Boys hold a persistent, 13-point math advantage on STAR — and it spans every grade.
Boys outscored girls in STAR Math in every grade level both years, with the gap ranging from 4 to 28 points. The district-wide gap stayed at ~13 points across both years, indicating a stable and structural difference in assessed math skill.
3
CAASPP tells a split story: girls lead in ELA proficiency, boys lead in Math by 7.5 points.
Girls' ELA proficiency (38.7%) exceeds boys' (34.8%) across grades 4–8. But boys' Math proficiency (30.5%) outpaces girls' (23.0%) — a 7.5-point gap. The Math gap is largest in 5th grade (+10.9 pts) and 8th grade (+11.3 pts).
4
Boys fail ELA at nearly twice the rate of girls in secondary school.
Among grades 6–8, 20.7% of boys received an F in ELA compared to 13.4% of girls — a 7.3-point gap. Boys also earn fewer A/B grades (44.3% vs 53.0%). Girls consistently earn more high grades across all secondary ELA courses.
5
The discipline gap is the largest single gap in this dataset — and it's concentrated in elementary.
Boys averaged 1.1 discipline incidents vs 0.3 for girls (a 15.3-point gap in incident rate). Elementary boys averaged 1.6 incidents compared to 0.3 for elementary girls. The gap shrinks dramatically in secondary (0.5 vs 0.3), suggesting early-grade intervention may be the highest-leverage target.
6
Attendance is nearly equal district-wide, but the chronic absenteeism pattern flips between levels.
Elementary boys are more chronically absent (15.1% vs 11.3% for elementary girls), but in secondary the pattern reverses — secondary girls are slightly more chronically absent (17.2% vs 15.6% for boys). Secondary girls' attendance warrants closer monitoring.
7
STAR and CAASPP scores align strongly — but attendance and discipline barely connect to academics.
STAR Reading and CAASPP ELA show a strong positive correlation, as do STAR Math and CAASPP Math. However, neither attendance nor discipline incidents showed meaningful correlation with academic scores for either gender, suggesting these are somewhat independent risk factors.
8
7th-grade girls show the steepest ELA advantage on CAASPP — but 6th-grade boys flip the script.
7th-grade girls outpace 7th-grade boys in ELA proficiency by 14.9 percentage points (46.9% vs 32.0%) — the largest single-grade ELA gap. Yet in 6th grade, boys actually lead girls in ELA proficiency by 8 points (45.3% vs 37.3%), making 6th grade the only middle school grade where boys hold an ELA edge.

Key Takeaways

  • 📋893 students analyzed across grades 2–8: 449 boys and 444 girls.
  • 📖STAR Reading: Boys grew +4.1 points year over year. Girls grew +0.7 points.
  • 🔢STAR Math: Boys changed -1.5 points year over year. Girls changed -0.7 points.
  • 📝CAASPP: Girls led in ELA proficiency (38.7% vs 34.8%). Boys led in Math (30.5% vs 23.0%).
  • ⚠️Discipline gap: Boys averaged 1.1 incidents vs 0.3 for girls. 26.8% of boys had at least one incident compared to 11.5% of girls.

How Measures Connect

This section examines whether different measures tend to rise and fall together across individual students — what statisticians call a correlation. A strong positive connection means students who score high on one measure also tend to score high on the other. Very weak / none means knowing one measure tells us almost nothing about the other. The most important finding here: STAR assessments and CAASPP results are strongly aligned (validating that STAR is a good predictor of state test performance), but attendance and discipline are essentially disconnected from academics — meaning academically struggling students are not the same as frequently absent or disciplined students, and vice versa.

Strength labels: Strong (r ≥ 0.5), Moderate (0.3–0.5), Weak (0.1–0.3), Very weak / none (<0.1). Where boys and girls show meaningfully different patterns, we call that out.
